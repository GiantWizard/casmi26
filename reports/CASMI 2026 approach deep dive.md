# Win the isomer tiebreak, not the candidate hunt

The remaining headroom in Enveda CASMI 2026 sits almost entirely inside same-formula isomer groups. Our own held-out natural-product numbers (truth in top-25 for 88%, MRR 0.525, 96% of misses being same-formula isomers) match the forum's independent decomposition, where truth sits at ranks 2 to 25 for 40% of class-2 molecules but is absent for only 1.6%, so ranking beats recall about 17:1 ([D742055](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742055)). The highest expected gain per unit of effort therefore comes from four moves, in order: build a count-matched, identity-disjoint validation harness so decisions stop riding on roughly 130 public molecules; route our fine-tuned GLACIER by adduct instead of stacking it as a second full-weight term; finish the leak-free LightGBM with within-formula-group features and a stacked fusion that preserves score margins RRF throws away; and clean up metric-identity hygiene (scorer keys, tautomers, valid tails). Contrastive fine-tuning of GLACIER on timsTOF data and an orthogonal second opinion (DreaMS head or retrained MIST) come next. De novo generation is last: every public leaderboard report on generated candidates is flat or negative, the only competitive released generator (FRIGID) ships CC BY-NC weights the hosts have not ruled on, and our own oracle puts realistic gains at +0.001 to +0.005. The current leader, at 0.484, says no extra data is needed and estimates a realistic ceiling near 0.55 ([D746302](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/746302)), which is consistent with a ranking-quality race rather than a data race.

## The public leaderboard resolves about one molecule per 0.008

Every prioritization decision depends on what the public leaderboard can and cannot tell us. Participants estimate the public split at about 130 to 132 molecules, so moving one molecule to rank 1 is worth roughly **0.0076 to 0.008**, identical reruns have differed by up to 0.007, and two byte-identical notebooks differed by 0.005 ([prvsiyan V33](https://www.kaggle.com/code/prvsiyan/analog-propagation-casmi-2026-baseline)). A participant with about 30 submissions reports that **offline gains below about 0.03 were invisible on the leaderboard** and that GBDT seed changes alone move holdout MRR by ±0.007 ([D743254](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/743254)). The sharpest critique on the forum argues that every step of the public 0.40 to 0.44 lineage after the early out-of-fold work was chosen on those ~130 molecules, with ICEBERG adopted at +0.013 and later dropped on a 0.402 vs 0.417 comparison, and no public identity-disjoint CV for the full fusion pipeline ([D748068](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/748068)). The private split is roughly twice the size, so public overfitting will be partly undone at the end.

Read against that floor, our own 0.438 to 0.414 drop from adding fine-tuned GLACIER as a second full-weight term is about three molecules. On its own that is weak evidence, but it points in the direction the literature predicts. In CASMI 2016, an over-weighted, correlated source (MoNA) added no Top-1 gain to an otherwise winning consensus ([Schymanski et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5368104/)), and MetFrag found an extra score helps only at a jointly tuned, sum-to-one weight ([MetFrag 2.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4732001/)). Two GLACIER variants with the same architecture and overlapping training data are close to the worst case for naive additive fusion. The fix is routing and learned weighting, not abandoning the fine-tune, whose non-[M+H]+ isomer gain (0.375 to 0.526 on our held-out set) is the largest single isomer-discrimination improvement we have measured.

The steps that look credible beyond noise are the ones that recur across independent authors or exceed 0.01: two-engine fusion (+0.013 to +0.014), the PubChem join or promotion of PubChem candidates (+0.011 to +0.024), restricting GLACIER to [M+H]+ or using it alone (+0.006 to +0.015, seen by several authors), pool popularity rerank (+0.008 to +0.010), and pairing merged and single FPNets (+0.019) ([huseyinemreaksoy](https://www.kaggle.com/code/huseyinemreaksoy/casmi26-v4n-fusion-pubchem-on-public-0-421), [wangpenghua v29](https://www.kaggle.com/code/wangpenghua/casmi26-v29-fusion-pop-glmh), [Gluzdov history](https://www.kaggle.com/code/dmitriigluzdov/casmi-26-from-spectra-to-structures), [prvsiyan V33](https://www.kaggle.com/code/prvsiyan/analog-propagation-casmi-2026-baseline)). The clear losses are just as informative: blind PubChem expansion (0.335 to 0.205), fragment rescoring applied to every molecule (−0.014), a wider class-3 generator (−0.024), a learned PubChem-first router (−0.069), and timsTOF fine-tuning of FPNet (0.337 to 0.328) ([wangpenghua v29](https://www.kaggle.com/code/wangpenghua/casmi26-v29-fusion-pop-glmh), [D742055](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742055)). The 0.433 to 0.440 public steps (window 40 vs 80, ICEBERG where GLACIER is blind, safe tails) are each about one molecule and should be treated as unproven.

The validation gap has a specific shape. Test molecules carry about **3.03 spectra each against 4.34 to 4.99 in typical CV**, and an aggregation feature that predicted +0.019 on unmatched CV shrank to +0.0065 once spectrum counts were matched, while its leaderboard move was +0.008 ([D742055](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742055)). The popularity prior shows the other trap: it lifted one team's class-2/3 CV from 0.11 to 0.61 while the leaderboard stayed at 0.176, because popularity alone puts the answer at rank 1 for 67% of enveda-np-examples but about 0% of Enveda-180 molecules lacking public spectra ([D748068](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/748068)). A harness that resamples to about three spectra per molecule, splits by identity (Gluzdov's 5-fold `fold_identity` over 277,566 structures is public ([dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/dmitriigluzdov/casmi26-identity-disjoint-validation-folds))), and reports famous and obscure molecules separately is the precondition for every item below.

## Forward simulation is the only separator that has moved the board

Seven hand-built same-formula separators (MetFrag-lite, connectivity scoring, formula gate, collision-energy depth, base neutral loss, ICEBERG as a feature, ring-cut infeasibility) each gave about 0 to +0.004, even though an oracle that removes same-formula blockers would take MRR from 0.7315 to 0.9869 ([D742055](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742055)). GLACIER is the one isomer signal that has repeatedly moved the public leaderboard, and the literature explains why. On MassSpecGym's formula challenge, the closest public analogue to our failure mode, **GLACIER reaches 49.88% Hit@1 with contrastive fine-tuning against 44.35% for ICEBERG 2.0, 34.03% for MARASON and 31.93% for FraGNNet**, at about 1.3 ms per spectrum versus 10.8 ms for ICEBERG 2.0 ([GLACIER arXiv 2606.29161](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29161)). Fingerprint retrieval models sit far lower on the same track (MIST 9.57%, Fingerprint FFN 5.09%) ([MassSpecGym](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.23326)), which is why the FPNet-centred ranker cannot settle isomer ties on its own.

The binding constraint is adduct coverage. MassSpecGym-trained ms-pred checkpoints see only [M+H]+ and [M+Na]+ (GLACIER [M+H]+ in public pipelines), while ICEBERG 2.0 and GLACIER cover negative mode and water-loss adducts only in their NIST-trained form, which is banned; no public model covers formate [M+CH2O2−H]− at all ([ICEBERG 2.0 bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.05.28.656653v1.full), [ms-pred README](https://github.com/coleygroup/ms-pred)). On the visible placeholder test, 29 of 400 molecules received no forward-model evidence ([Gluzdov notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/dmitriigluzdov/casmi-26-from-spectra-to-structures)), and no public post measures negative-mode performance separately. Our fine-tuned GLACIER is therefore the only non-NIST forward signal we know of for [M+NH4]+, [M+K]+, water-loss, [M−H]−, [M+Cl]− and formate spectra. That makes adduct routing (base GLACIER for [M+H]+, fine-tuned GLACIER everywhere base GLACIER is blind, replacing the ICEBERG-where-GLACIER-is-blind fallback that samanyu's 0.438 notebook uses ([samanyu1808](https://www.kaggle.com/code/samanyu1808/casmi26-iceberg-where-glacier-is-blind-lb-0-438))) the cheapest test with the clearest mechanism. It keeps the [M+H]+ result at 0.718 by construction and targets exactly the subset where our measured gain was largest. How many hidden-test molecules fall into that subset is unknown; the visible placeholder's 79% [M+H]+ and 16% [M−H]− describe train-derived dummy rows, not the hidden set ([D744011](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/744011), [D741404](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/741404)).

Beyond routing, three forward-model levers have published support. First, contrastive fine-tuning with PubChem same-formula decoys is the documented retrieval-specific lever, but its published effect on MassSpecGym is modest (48.49% to 49.88% Hit@1) and it costs a little spectral cosine ([GLACIER](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.29161)); applied to our timsTOF fine-tune it is worth a measured test, not an assumption of a large jump. Second, the similarity function is a free knob: entropy similarity beat 42 alternatives for experimental library matching ([Li et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41592-021-01331-z)), while the forward-model papers rank by binned cosine, and no study compares them for predicted-versus-measured spectra, so computing both and letting the ranker choose is cheap. Third, a third forward model is available but likely correlated. FIORA trained only on MSnLib (CC BY 4.0, MIT code) is already packaged for Kaggle with no leaderboard report ([ahmedberatozer/casmi26-fiora-os-v1](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ahmedberatozer/casmi26-fiora-os-v1)), and CFM-ID 4 is host-approved ([D743774](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/743774)) with QTOF CID training closer to timsTOF and true [M−H]− support, but at roughly a CPU-minute per molecule it is top-10-only ([CFM-ID 4.0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9252813/)). ICEBERG's track record here is contested (+0.007 in one lineage, −0.013 when switched off in another, +0.015 when dropped in favour of GLACIER alone), and the most plausible reconciliation, not tested by anyone, is that a second forward model helps when it is the only one and adds noise on top of GLACIER ([evgendvorkin lineage](https://www.kaggle.com/code/evgendvorkin/enveda-casmi-2026), [wangpenghua v29](https://www.kaggle.com/code/wangpenghua/casmi26-v29-fusion-pop-glmh), [prvsiyan V33](https://www.kaggle.com/code/prvsiyan/analog-propagation-casmi-2026-baseline)). That same logic predicts our 0.414 result and argues for measuring error correlation before adding any further forward score.

## Rank fusion is discarding the margins that separate isomers

The 0.42 to 0.44 public family fuses engines with weighted reciprocal-rank fusion, `1/(3+r_main) + 0.6/(3+r_eng2)`, then reranks same-formula groups with `z + 1.0·z(ICE) + 1.0·z(GL)` ([ahmedberatozer v6a](https://www.kaggle.com/code/ahmedberatozer/casmi26-v6a-glonly-fusion)). RRF is robust when scores are uncalibrated ([Cormack et al. 2009](https://plg.uwaterloo.ca/~gvcormac/cormacksigir09-rrf.pdf)), but for MRR the decisive question is which isomer goes first, and RRF throws away the score gap that answers it. Thirty years of metabolite-ID fusion point the same way: normalize each score within the candidate list, fit weights jointly on held-out data with a sum-to-one constraint, and let per-query context speak. COSMIC found the **score gap to the runner-up and the number of candidates** among the most informative confidence features, enforced monotone directionality so a higher similarity can never lower confidence, and showed raw CSI:FingerID scores were worse than random on a new instrument's data while calibrated scores still worked ([Hoffmann et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8926923/)). Per-group z-scores, as the public stack uses, are unstable when an isomer group has two to five members; shrinking z toward zero for small groups, or using rank-within-group and max-normalized scores alongside it, is the standard remedy (estimate; no paper compares these head-to-head for MS/MS).

This is where our in-progress leak-free LightGBM matters most, and the public evidence says the leak is real. Gluzdov's identity-held-out FPNet scores MRR@25 0.790 on unseen identities but only **0.506 on the timsTOF natural-product panel**, while the public FPNet A scores 0.934 on queries it trained on and 0.581 on the panel ([fold-safe FPNet](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/dmitriigluzdov/casmi26-fold-safe-fpnet)). A ranker trained on rows scored by a full-train FPNet learns to over-trust the fingerprint channel, which is the inflation megayak flagged on seen versus held-out libraries ([megayak](https://www.kaggle.com/code/megayak/casmi26-two-rankers-one-engine)). The design that follows from the evidence is a cascade: the existing recall stage, the leak-free LightGBM, then a dedicated isomer stage trained only on same-formula groups that contain the truth, with a listwise softmax objective that targets top-1 within group, and features expressed as within-group differences (score minus group max, rank, gap to second, group size) for FPNet, both GLACIER variants, ICEBERG, popularity and any new signal. If the second GLACIER is kept as a feature rather than routed, feeding its residual after regressing on base GLACIER gives the model only the new information (estimate). A stacked logistic or LightGBM fusion over both engines' out-of-fold scores should replace hand-weighted RRF once the harness can show it wins.

Three lower-effort hygiene items belong in the same pass because each is worth a molecule or two at near-zero risk. About 8% of holdout molecules had a tautomer copy of the truth under a different PubChem key ([D743254](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/743254)); the shipped `inchikey14` differs from the scorer key for about 4.4% of NP structures and about 10% of COCONUT keys change under scorer canonicalisation ([D742042](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742042), [megayak](https://www.kaggle.com/code/megayak/casmi26-two-rankers-one-engine)); and the metric now applies `RemoveStereochemistry` ([D746878](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/746878)). Deduplicating after ranking on the official metric identity is exactly what obstacledeveloper's V1.1 did ([V1.1](https://www.kaggle.com/code/obstacledeveloper/casmi26-v1-1-metric-audit-and-safe-tail)). There is also an unexplained grader behaviour: with identical rank-1 answers, different valid ranks 2 to 25 scored 0.388 to 0.397 against 0.426 ([D748029](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/748029)), and junk padding scored 0.000 twice ([D742088](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742088)). Until the hosts explain it, tails should be RDKit-valid, deduplicated, neutral single-fragment SMILES, and tail changes should be tested in isolation.

## Orthogonal evidence and priors help only where they disagree with GLACIER

The literature's strongest ensembling lesson is that gains came from combining methodologically different evidence, while voting over similar tools did not beat the best single tool on validation ([Schymanski et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5368104/), [Blaženović et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5445034/)). The candidates for a genuinely different second opinion, filtered for permissive licences, are a short list. DreaMS weights are CC BY 4.0 and host-approved ([Zenodo 10997887](https://zenodo.org/records/10997887), [D745604](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/745604)), but as an analog channel it only ties classical mass-shifted search (0.58 vs 0.57 to 0.63 on class 2) ([megayak](https://www.kaggle.com/code/megayak/casmi26-two-rankers-one-engine)) and placed the truth closer than the blocker in 20 of 42 cases, a coin flip ([D742055](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742055)); its value would come from a trained structure head, not kNN. MIST is MIT-licensed with permitted NPLIB1 weights, [M+H]+ only, and MIST trained with leakage-free ICEBERG augmentation reaches **53.76% formula-bonus Hit@1**, the best audited retrieval number, though no released checkpoint for that configuration was confirmed (UNVERIFIED) ([MIST](https://github.com/samgoldman97/mist), [MassSpecGym in the Wild](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19624v1)). JESTR is MIT with MassSpecGym weights but reaches only 11.8% on the formula track, and FLARE's 22.66% comes without verified code ([JESTR](https://github.com/HassounLab/JESTR), [FLARE](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.01.27.702086.full.pdf)). The June 2026 audit found evaluation problems in at least 17 of 26 MassSpecGym papers, and showed a DreaMS + ChemBERTa method falling from 82.41% to 6.70% once a SMILES-canonicalization shortcut was fixed ([arXiv 2606.19624](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19624v1)), so SpecBridge's and GLMR's 68 to 85% claims should be treated as about 7% until shown otherwise. Any model that reads SMILES must see candidates canonicalized identically to the truth.

Priors are double-edged in a measurable way. In CASMI 2016, ChemSpider reference count alone produced 143 Top-1 answers and beat the combined fragmenters ([Schymanski et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5368104/)); in CASMI 2017, adding source counts to CFM-ID lowered top-5 because the answers were obscure ([EPA retrospective](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7345619/)). The Kaggle data reproduces both regimes: on Gluzdov's 250-molecule NP panel, FPNet alone scores 0.51 and FPNet plus 0.25 × popularity scores 0.95 to 0.97 ([NP panel](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/dmitriigluzdov/casmi26-natural-product-panel-benchmark)), yet popularity is near-useless on Enveda-180 molecules without public spectra ([D748068](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/748068)), and the hosts hope the test holds "enough novel structures to make it worth working on them" ([D745758](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/745758)). Enveda's own EnvedaDark benchmark is 226 NPs absent from major databases ([MS2Mol](https://chemrxiv.org/engage/chemrxiv/article-details/6492507524989702c2b082fc)). The implication is that popularity weight should be learned conditionally (on retrieval confidence, library-hit strength and group size) rather than fixed at 0.15 to 0.25. Organism-free NP priors such as RDKit NP-likeness or a plant-origin flag have only small, old evidence ([NP-likeness](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4089557/)), and the strong taxonomic prior (top-1 10.2% to about 72%) needs the source organism, which the test does not provide ([Rutz et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6824209/)). Ion mobility and retention time are dead ends: the hosts confirmed the test has no CCS or 1/K0 ([D743368](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/743368)), and even the best CCS predictors' ~1.2% median error is about the size of isomer CCS differences ([SigmaCCS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10319785/)).

## Recall and novel structures are a thin tail

With truth absent for only 1.6% of class-2 held-out molecules and beyond rank 25 for 2.4% ([D742055](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742055)), recall work has little room unless the hidden test is far more novel than the CV sets. The cheap recall adds are host-approved reference spectra rather than new structure databases. Enveda-180 on Zenodo carries **816 InChIKey14s with standard-adduct spectra absent from train.parquet** (CC BY 4.0, allowed) ([D745148](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/745148)), and newer GNPS, MassBank and MoNA releases plus GNPS-NIST14-MATCHES are allowed ([D745228](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/745228), [D745832](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/745832)); a plain library search scores MRR@25 0.907 on NPs that have reference spectra ([D741404](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/741404)). More NP structure databases add nothing: ChEBI, LIPID MAPS and NPAtlas added no coverage beyond PubChem ∪ COCONUT, and membership flags hurt on the leaderboard ([D743254](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/743254)), which fits COCONUT 2.0 already aggregating 63 collections and 695,133 unique structures ([COCONUT 2.0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11701633/)).

Class 3, by the hosts' definition, is absent from both PubChem and COCONUT ([D742274](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742274)), and a participant's LB-probe estimate puts the class mix near 16% / 45% / 39% ([D741597](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/741597), participant estimate), which yields a retrieval-only ceiling of about 0.61 ([D742088](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742088)). That 39% looks like an invitation to generate, but the measured record says otherwise. The audited MassSpecGym de novo table gives **FRIGID 18.29% top-1 and 22.00% top-10 with the formula known**, dropping to 14.28% / 17.43% with MIST-CF top-5 formulas, while every other audited method sits at or below 2.3% top-1 except a corrected MolForge at 10.73% ([MassSpecGym in the Wild](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19624), [FRIGID](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16648)); unaudited claims above 20% (MS-GPT, DualLGD, MSFlow) share the MIST encoder lineage the audit found contaminated in one checkpoint, so they are UNVERIFIED for planning. Removing Tanimoto ≥ 0.7 analogs from decoder pretraining cuts exact-match accuracy by about 30% relative (11.6% to 8.0%) ([audit Table 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.19624)), so scaffold-novel test molecules are likely near zero for any generator. On Kaggle, a wider class-3 generator cost 0.024, derivative candidates cost 0.002, and deleting-truth simulations overstated generator reach about threefold ([wangpenghua v29](https://www.kaggle.com/code/wangpenghua/casmi26-v29-fusion-pop-glmh), [D742055](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/742055), [D743254](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/743254)). Our oracle's realistic +0.001 to +0.005 is consistent with all of this.

FRIGID is also the only generator near the competitive range, and it carries the worst compliance profile. Its code is Apache 2.0, but the only checkpoints (Zenodo 19685145, 2.8 GB) are CC BY-NC 4.0, and the forum question on them is unanswered ([FRIGID GitHub](https://github.com/coleygroup/FRIGID), [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/19685145), [D746430](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/746430)). The backbone takes 6.58 s per spectrum on an RTX 6000 Ada and 86.49 s with ICEBERG refinement ([FRIGID](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16648)); on a T4 that is very roughly 30 to 50 s and 7 to 12 min respectively (estimate), so only backbone sampling on a gated low-confidence subset fits a 9-hour budget. If generation is used at all, the zero-slot-risk version is to use the generated set as a reranking signal for existing retrieval candidates (similarity of each candidate to the generated structures) rather than inserting new SMILES, an idea consistent with FRIGID's 45.27% generative-retrieval Hit@1 but not measured on Kaggle (estimate).

## Prioritized action plan with go/no-go gates

Expected gains below are this report's estimates on the private leaderboard unless marked as measured, and every gate assumes the count-matched harness from item 1 exists. A "go" means promote to a scored submission; a public leaderboard result alone never overrides a harness failure, and a public drop of more than about two molecules (0.016) after a harness win should trigger a re-check for train/test mismatch rather than an automatic revert.

## Conclusion

The field's history and this competition's forum converge on one diagnosis: formula is nearly solved (about 94 to 95% in CASMI 2022, over 95% here), while structure within a formula is the bottleneck, and the only lever with both published and leaderboard support is forward simulation, used carefully. The new insight from combining our numbers with the public record is that our 0.414 setback was a fusion error rather than a model error: the fine-tuned GLACIER owns a subset (non-[M+H]+ spectra) where no permissive alternative exists, and the right move is to give it that subset outright while a leak-free, margin-preserving ranker learns how much to trust each channel elsewhere.

The decisive constraint is epistemic. A public split of ~130 molecules cannot rank changes worth less than about 0.02, the private split will roughly double the sample, and the leader's 0.55 ceiling estimate implies the top teams are separated by a few dozen correct isomer calls. A team that chooses its final two submissions from a count-matched harness (one best-harness model, one conservative diverse ensemble) is betting on the private split; a team that chooses from the public board is betting on noise. Two dates matter and are UNVERIFIED: a participant reports a December 7 team-merge deadline and a December 14 final-submission deadline ([D741359](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/discussion/741359)), which should be confirmed on the official rules page before planning around them.
